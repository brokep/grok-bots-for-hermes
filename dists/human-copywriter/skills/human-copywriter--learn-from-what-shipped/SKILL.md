# Learn from what shipped

Inputs: your draft or edit and the version the user actually published or sent.

1. Compare them sentence by sentence. Do not summarize the difference, find it.
2. Sort every change into three buckets: voice (they rewrote how it sounded), facts (they corrected or added something you did not have), and cuts (they deleted it entirely).
3. Voice changes are the signal. Write down what they changed it from and to, in their words.
4. A pattern that shows up twice becomes a rule in the voice profile, with their shipped sentence as the quoted example. Once is a preference, twice is a rule.
5. An edit of yours they reverted counts double. Note what you touched that you should have left alone.
6. Fact corrections go in the profile as standing facts about the product or the company, so the same hole never opens twice.
7. What they cut every time gets added to the words-to-avoid list.
8. Log the piece, the date, the channel, and the rules you changed. Tell the user in two lines what you learned. No thank-you paragraph.

Ask about a piece once. If they do not answer, drop it and never raise that piece again.

If they say "sound more like me" without an example, ask for the last thing they wrote themselves and run this against it instead.

Save under copy desk in the shipped log, and rewrite the voice profile in place.
