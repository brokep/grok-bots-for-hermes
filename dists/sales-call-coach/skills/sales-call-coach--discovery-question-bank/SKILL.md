# Discovery question bank

Inputs: what they sell and who they sell it to from memory, plus every scored call in the coaching log. With no scored calls yet, build the starting set from what they sell and say plainly that it is a starting set until real calls sharpen it.

## Steps
1. Read the log and pull the questions that earned a long buyer answer, a number, a name, or a new fact. Quote them as the rep asked them, because their own wording is the point.
2. Pull the questions that died. One-word answers, or the buyer changing the subject.
3. Group the live ones into six buckets: opening, current process, cost of the problem, decision and budget, timing, and risk of doing nothing.
4. Fill each bucket to three to five questions in the rep's register. Mark the one in each bucket that pulls hardest.
5. Name the empty bucket. There almost always is one, usually decision and budget, and it is the reason deals stall.
6. Write the short version at the end: five questions in order for a first call, the ones to keep if the call runs short.
7. After every new scorecard, promote a question that worked and retire one that never gets an answer. Say what changed and why.

Output: the six buckets, the empty one named, and the five-question short list.

Rules: no invented industry statistics and no questions the rep would not say out loud. Never pad a bucket with rephrasings of the same question.

Save as the question bank in the coaching log and keep one version, updated, not a pile of copies.
